Master Alavandor;

I worry this note will find you at a desperate hour, and indeed, if
you're reading where I left it, surely that is the case.

I read something the other day that put your family history into my
thoughts. It was the latest edition of the Booke of Surveys and Measures
for Thistle Ridge. It differed from the 1436 edition in all the ways you
would expect; Thistle Ridge has been prospering these last years, so all
the things the Survey is meant to measure have increased. The
discrepancy is not in the numbers, and though I won't name it, I fear
our enemies will see it and act upon it.

This discovery has led me to reading a relevant volume to brush up on
history, and I will be writing a forward to it so that you may know it's
relevance if it comes across your desk.

I hope that I am mistaken that you'll benefit from this letter, but it
has been my misfortune to be correct in these matters, even though I
don't seek out this knowledge, and am reticent to share it. I sometimes
worry about the self-fulfilling prophecy, but this is a topic for a
discussion in person sometime, with a warm cup of tea.

Being unsure exactly why one changed entry in a publication otherwise
known for its exactitude would set me to worry, I won't make further
speculations. What I know is this; there is a friend of mine that could
help you.

His name is Anavex, a Chessentan monk of great character and manners.
You will be familiar with his people, and he will no doubt recognize
you. He is nearby where I write this letter, some two miles east, in the
terrible shadow-copy of an otherwise perfect apple orchard, the fruit of
which I've enjoyed for decades.

Anavex has been bound to the shadow-orchard for many years now, and we
talk from time to time. His is a strange prison, exacerbated by the
oriental vows of his order. We talk mainly in riddles, as his
requirement to be plain with me I cannot meet.

When you come to him, he will tell you what must be done. Do not refuse
him. What he asks, he asks as a mercy to himself, and he will ask it
because I have sent you. What he gives in return will be worth the
weight of it, and more.

I will offer you a token of good faith, so that when you stand before
Anavex and are uncertain, you may know my word was truthful. Tell him
that his brother, Aravex, is alive, which I know from first-hand account
of a reliable woman whom we trust.

He will understand. This fact has been a comfort to me in the carrying,
and I expect it will be a greater comfort to him in the hearing. I have
wanted him to know it for a long time, but my own constraints have kept
me from saying it plainly in that place. If you can deliver it for me, I
will consider myself in your debt.

I will pray for you, for whatever small good that may do. If we do not
meet in this life, I will be happy to take tea with you Hereafter.

Until then I am,

Your servant,

Phin Hoetmer.

27 Flamerule, 1440 DR.

Yellow Abbey, Dagger Falls.
